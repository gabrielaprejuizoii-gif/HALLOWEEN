to summon · to cast a spell — to call something to appear · to use magic words. She cast a spell on the village.

IV · Reading & Culture · 10 min

Witches: Myth, History & Fear

Before you read: pointy hat, broom, cauldron… where does this image come from? Is it true?

📖 Source: https://www.history.com/articles/history-of-witches

Biblical times1 Samuel: King Saul asks the Witch of Endor to summon the spirit of the prophet Samuel.
1400sWitch hysteria grows in medieval Europe. Many accused confessed, often under torture.
1486Malleus Maleficarum (“The Hammer of Witches”): a guide to identifying, hunting and interrogating witches.
1500–1660Up to 80,000 suspects executed in Europe, around 80% of them women.
1647Alse Young: first person executed for witchcraft in the American colonies.
1692Salem: about 150 accused, 19 executed. Six of those executed were men.
1706–1730Grace Sherwood’s “water test”; a satirical article mocks witch trials. Mania fades.
TodayMany witches practise Wicca (“harm none”). Persecution of people accused of witchcraft still happens in some places.

Check your understanding

1. Who were many accused “witches” in reality?
2. What was Malleus Maleficarum?
3. Why did panic spread in Salem?
4. What do modern Wiccans believe?

Fact, belief or interpretation?

a) Salem’s trials were in 1692.
b) Witches served the Devil.
c) Witch hunts were a way to control people on society’s margins.

Answers (factual): 1) Natural healers, “wise women”, misunderstood. 2) 1486 manual for hunting and interrogating witches. 3) Mass hysteria in a tense climate: wars, smallpox, fear, scapegoating. 4) “Harm none”, peace, balance with nature; Book of Shadows. Sorting: a = fact, b = belief, c = interpretation (accept reasoned alternatives). Never say that all the accused practised magic or that all victims were women.
V · Culture & Society · 12 min

Witches & Women: Who was feared?

According to the article, single women, widows and women on the margins of society were especially targeted. Here is the big question: was fear of witches also fear of women who didn’t fit the rules?

Then

Women who were healers, poor, alone, or outspoken could be easily blamed.

A turning point

Later writers and activists began to reclaim the witch as a symbol of female independence and resistance.
(Interpretation, not from the article. Discuss it critically.)

Now

From fairy-tale villain to empowered heroine on screen and in books. Real accusations still exist.

Why might a society fear women who are independent, knowledgeable or different?
Is “witch” still used today as an insult for powerful women? Where do you see it?
Think of women in Gilmore Girls, Sex and the City, 13 Going on 30 or Uptown Girls. How do they challenge expectations?
Have witches become empowering, or have we simplified them? What’s the difference between being feared and being misunderstood?

Academic words

social stigma — disapproval of a groupscapegoat — a person blamed for others’ problemspersecution — cruel treatment for identity or beliefsmass hysteria — shared panicto reclaim — to take back and give new meaningcultural representation — how a group is shown
Goal: abstract argumentation, social analysis. Be careful: the article gives data (women were most of those executed; single women and widows were targeted), but linking witch hunts to women’s emancipation is an interpretation. Make that distinction explicit with her. Open paths: scapegoating (Girard), economic and social control, Gilmore/Sex and the City as modern “unruly women”, risk of commercialising the “witch” image. Keep teacher talking under 30%.
VI · Immersion · 7 min

The Witching Hour: What Would You Do?

It’s 11:47 p.m. You’re staying in an old cottage near a forest. The lights suddenly go out. You hear footsteps upstairs, even though you’re certain you’re alone. On the table, there’s an old book with your name written on its first page.

You hear someone whispering your name. What do you do?
The book opens by itself. Do you read it or walk away?
You find a photograph of someone who looks exactly like you. How do you react?
You discover that the previous owner disappeared mysteriously. What do you investigate first?

Clues

Hypotheses

If I were in that situation, I would…My first instinct would be to…It is possible that…I suspect that…What makes this even more unsettling is…Had I known that…, I would have…

Read the scenario aloud and ask her to describe the room before any decision. The phrases are a quiet safety net, not a fill-in exercise. Open paths: rational explanations (wind, prank, previous owner) vs. supernatural; the look-alike photo (ancestor, hoax, doppelgänger). Note which structures she uses naturally.
